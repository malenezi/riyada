# Round terms for founders

Plain explanations of what early-stage founders are asked to sign. Legal detail differs by country and by document; a lawyer in the company's jurisdiction should review anything before signature. This is not legal or investment advice.

## Valuation and dilution

- **Pre-money valuation**: the agreed value of the company before the new money.
- **Post-money valuation**: pre-money plus the investment.
- **Investor ownership** = investment / post-money.
- **Founder dilution**: every existing holder's percentage is multiplied by (1 - investor ownership).

Worked example: raise 2,000,000 at 8,000,000 pre-money.

| | Value |
|---|---|
| Post-money | 10,000,000 |
| Investor ownership | 2,000,000 / 10,000,000 = 20% |
| A founder who held 50% now holds | 50% x (1 - 20%) = 40% |

If an option pool is created or enlarged **before** the round (as investors often request), it dilutes existing holders only. A 10% pre-round pool in the example above takes the founder from 50% to 45% before the round, then to 36% after it.

Run this arithmetic across the next two rounds as well. Ownership after seed matters less than ownership after the round that follows.

## Instruments

| Instrument | What it is | Points to understand |
|---|---|---|
| Priced equity round | Investors buy new shares at an agreed price | Sets a valuation; comes with a shareholder agreement and investor rights |
| Convertible note | A loan that converts to shares at a later priced round | Has interest and a maturity date; ask what happens at maturity if no round occurs |
| SAFE or similar agreement | A right to future shares, not a loan | No interest or maturity; check whether the cap is pre-money or post-money, because post-money caps make each additional SAFE dilute the founders, not the other SAFE holders |
| Grant or non-dilutive programme | Money without ownership | Read reporting duties, intellectual property clauses and clawback conditions |
| Venture debt or revenue-based financing | Borrowing repaid from future revenue | Suits predictable revenue; check covenants and personal guarantees |

Local equivalents and their enforceability vary. Confirm which instruments are recognised where the company is incorporated.

## Conversion terms

- **Valuation cap**: the highest valuation at which the note or SAFE converts. Lower cap, more shares for the investor.
- **Discount**: a percentage off the next round's price. The investor usually gets whichever of cap or discount gives more shares.
- **Most favoured nation**: if later investors get better terms before conversion, this investor gets them too.

## Economic terms in priced rounds

- **Liquidation preference**: on a sale or wind-up, preferred investors are paid first. "1x non-participating" means they choose between getting their money back or converting to ordinary shares. "Participating" means they get their money back and then share in the rest; it shifts value from founders in mid-sized exits.
- **Anti-dilution**: adjusts an investor's price if a later round is cheaper. "Broad-based weighted average" is the moderate form; "full ratchet" resets fully and is severe for founders.
- **Pro-rata right**: the right to invest in later rounds to keep the same percentage.
- **Option pool**: shares reserved for employees. Size it from a hiring plan, not a round number.

## Control terms

- **Board composition**: who appoints directors and who holds the deciding vote.
- **Protective provisions or reserved matters**: decisions that need investor consent, such as issuing shares, selling the company, taking debt, changing the business.
- **Information rights**: what reports investors receive and when.
- **Drag-along**: a majority can require everyone to sell. **Tag-along**: minorities can join a sale on the same terms.
- **Founder vesting**: founders earn their shares over time, commonly four years with a one-year cliff; unvested shares return to the company if a founder leaves. Check what happens on a sale ("acceleration").
- **Exclusivity or no-shop**: after signing a term sheet, the company may not negotiate with others for a set period.

## Reading a term sheet

1. Compute ownership after the round, including the option pool and all convertibles.
2. Model three exits: small, medium, large. Work out what founders receive under the liquidation preference.
3. List which decisions will need investor consent.
4. Note what is binding now (usually exclusivity, confidentiality, costs) and what is not.
5. Ask a lawyer about everything you cannot explain to a co-founder in one sentence.

## Diligence, in practice

Investors check that what the deck claims is true: the company owns its intellectual property, the revenue is real, the cap table is clean, contracts and licences exist, and there are no undisclosed disputes. Increasingly they also ask how AI is used: which models and providers, what data they receive, who approves agent actions, and what evaluation results show. Keep the approval matrix, eval results and incident log current; they answer most of these questions.
