# Fundraising

Investors fund evidence of progress and a believable plan for the next stage. Preparation is mostly making that evidence easy to check: one story, numbers that agree everywhere, documents ready before they are requested.

## Workflow

1. **Decide the round.** Before any slide: what milestones must the next 18 to 24 months reach, what do they cost (from the financial model's lowest cash point plus a margin), and is equity the right source? Lay out the alternatives: revenue, grants, non-dilutive programmes, debt. State the amount as a range tied to milestones.
2. **Check readiness.** Ask what exists: traction figures, financial model, cap table, incorporation documents. Missing essentials are the first task, not the deck.
3. **Write the storyline** in `assets/pitch-deck-outline`: one governing message, then eleven slide titles written as full sentences. Read the titles alone; they should make the case. Only then draft slide content. To build actual slides, hand the outline to a presentation skill.
4. **Build the pipeline** in `assets/investor-pipeline.csv`. Research each investor's stage, ticket size, sectors and recent investments from their own site and announcements; record the source. Rank by fit and by the strength of the introduction path. Thirty well-matched names beat two hundred.
5. **Prepare the data room** with `assets/data-room-checklist`. Flag what is missing and what needs a lawyer.
6. **Rehearse questions.** Draft the ten hardest questions for this company with two-sentence answers and the backup for each.
7. **After each meeting**, update the pipeline and draft the follow-up. Once the round closes, and monthly thereafter, use `assets/investor-update`.

Terms an early-stage founder meets, with the arithmetic of dilution: [references/round-terms.md](references/round-terms.md).

Use the template in the user's language (`.en.md` or `.ar.md`).

## Rules

- Every number in the deck matches the financial model and the data room. Check, and list any mismatch.
- No invented traction, customers, partners, commitments or investor interest. Do not imply that an investor is "close" unless the founder says so.
- Market figures on slides carry source and date.
- Do not fabricate facts about a named investor. Research them; if you cannot verify, say so.
- Outreach to investors is drafted for the founder to send. Investors back people; messages come from the founder's own account and in their voice.
- Explain terms and their effect on ownership and control; do not tell the founder which terms to accept or what the company is worth. Term sheets, shareholder agreements and securities rules need a qualified lawyer in the company's jurisdiction.
- Share only what the stage requires. Remove personal data and anything covered by a confidentiality clause before it enters the data room.
- Bad news goes into investor updates first and plainly.
- Reply in the user's language. Arabic terms follow `references/glossary.md`.

## Output

Depending on the stage: a round rationale (amount, milestones, alternatives considered); a filled deck outline with appendix list; a ranked pipeline with sources; a data-room status table; a question bank; or a finished investor update.
